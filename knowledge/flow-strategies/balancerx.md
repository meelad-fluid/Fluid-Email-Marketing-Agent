BalanceRx Email + SMS Flow Strategy
Strategic Direction
Build a conversion-focused lifecycle that moves prospects from signup to assessment start, assessment submission, and completed enrollment. Lead with transparent pricing, a simple online process, clinician review, and convenience. Use brief explanations to remove objections and support the next action.
The strategy retains nine flows and 27 email positions, with seven proposed SMS positions. SMS is coordinated with email for separately opted-in recipients, subject to approval of the business use case by the messaging provider. Existing patient, billing, and fulfillment communications remain the foundation of the post-enrollment experience.
Strategic Priorities
	•	Ask for assessment action in the first welcome email and throughout acquisition.
	•	Show the full plan price, qualifying duration, payment schedule, and included services together. Confirm current terms before using numerical pricing.
	•	Recover high-intent assessment and payment abandonment with direct, specific messages and secure links.
	•	Respond to behavior in Customer.io: progression ends the current journey and activates the next relevant stage.
	•	Use approved medication-specific email branches when a permitted interest signal supports them; keep a general fallback.
	•	Preserve clinical review, patient privacy, fulfillment accuracy, renewal rules, and suppression logic.
Brand and Messaging Direction
Use confident, concise copy and one primary action per message. Lead with the practical value of Balance: understand the price, complete the online assessment, receive clinician review, and manage next steps conveniently through the secure experience.
Starting or paying does not guarantee a prescription. Match every message to Balance’s actual assessment, approval, and payment order. Medication availability and suitability remain subject to clinician review and applicable requirements. Use approved medication descriptions and safety disclosures; compounded preparations are not FDA-approved. Do not promise outcomes, invent urgency, imply guaranteed approval, or describe compounded products as equivalent to FDA-approved medicines.

Acquisition Journey Rules
The timings below are initial test settings, measured from the specified event or last activity. They apply only while the relevant action remains incomplete. Check current status before every send; do not run a fixed sequence after someone progresses.
Priority: payment completion > assessment recovery > program exploration > welcome > general site abandonment. Maintain one active acquisition/recovery journey per person. Program interest can move someone out of welcome into exploration; assessment start ends all pre-assessment flows. Assessment submission ends recovery. While clinician review is pending, suppress sales recovery and use existing status communications. Payment recovery starts only when a valid payment step actually exists.

Coordinated SMS + Email Recovery
SMS is a planned recovery channel for separately opted-in recipients, with availability conditional on provider acceptance of Balance’s business and messaging use case. Customer.io’s SMS addendum prohibits prescription-drug offers in the US/Canada and includes business-type restrictions; neutral wording does not override them. Obtain explicit confirmation of a permitted service-reminder use case or an alternative approved route; do not send prohibited promotional offers. Confirm the permitted route before activating SMS. If unavailable, launch the email journeys as designed and retain these SMS positions for an approved provider integration.

Remove SMS after opt-out without withdrawing email permission unless the person requests both. Technical or medical support requests pause sales recovery until resolved.
Customer.io Behavioral Orchestration
Use events to control entry, progression, branching, and exit. Delays are inactivity windows and fallback spacing; they do not determine a person’s stage. Configure explicit exit conditions for every campaign, with conversion goals plus current-state filters. Do not assume an event-triggered campaign exits automatically after a later conversion.

Recommended implementation:
	•	Maintain minimal approved lifecycle state, channel consent, latest activity timestamp, and opaque attempt identifier. Avoid medication names in link parameters or analytics payloads unless explicitly approved.
	•	Use per-attempt deduplication and timestamp ordering so retries or late events cannot reactivate completed journeys. A genuinely new assessment can re-enter; the same attempt cannot repeatedly restart recovery.
	•	Reset inactivity timing when verified activity resumes. Revalidate current status immediately before each message, including after a quiet-hour delay.
	•	Use true/false or multi-branch logic for stage, permitted program interest, pricing interest, verified payment failure, and support status. If the medication branch is unavailable, retain a conversion-led general version.
	•	Apply conversion exits and explicit filters in Customer.io. Pair reporting goals to the relevant attempt where supported.
	•	When a link is clicked but no action follows, verify the secure destination and current state; route to the relevant objection/support branch rather than sending an unrelated introduction. Use clicks and backend actions rather than opens as behavioral evidence.
	•	Pause affected recovery journeys if completion/status feeds fail or become stale. Reconcile authoritative status before resuming.
Medication-Specific Journeys
Build program exploration and approved welcome/recovery body variants for semaglutide interest, tirzepatide interest, and general/unknown interest. These are content branches within the existing email positions, not additional flows or a recommendation of medication.
For a permitted explicitly selected program, show its verified current pricing and plan terms, relevant process FAQs, clinician-review requirement, applicable safety disclosures, and assessment CTA. A page visit alone does not establish eligibility or a prescription. Avoid clinical comparisons, outcome guarantees, unapproved equivalence claims, and automatic medication switching. Keep subjects, previews, SMS, and lock-screen content discreet. Use a general branch whenever permission, provider support, or approved medication content is missing.
Launch Priorities
Phase 1 — Conversion and recovery: Confirm the actual clinical/payment sequence and existing communications. Implement signup, assessment-first welcome, assessment recovery, and payment recovery with event-based exits. Activate the coordinated SMS positions through an approved route once consent, provider eligibility, and delivery controls are confirmed.
Phase 2 — Relevant prospect conversion: Add site abandonment and program exploration, including approved medication-specific variants. Test plan transparency and process-led objections using verified behavioral signals.
Phase 3 — Patient continuity: Preserve onboarding and fulfillment logic. Coordinate with existing account, dispatch, delivery, and care messages; add only uncovered steps. Preserve due-date-driven check-ins and renewal logic.
Phase 4 — Optimization and retention: Refine acquisition tests, eligible former-patient re-engagement, and sunset using completed actions and incremental results.

Initial Tests
	•	Welcome: transparent pricing versus online convenience as the lead benefit; assessment remains the primary CTA in both.
	•	Assessment recovery: first email after 30 minutes versus one hour of inactivity.
	•	Payment recovery: concise plan/price recap versus billing-objection support.
	•	Recovery channel mix: email-only versus coordinated email + SMS among SMS-eligible recipients.
	•	Program exploration: permitted medication-specific body content versus general program content.
Test one major variable at a time. Choose winners on submitted assessments or completed valid payment steps and downstream enrollment, with opt-outs and complaints as guardrails. Do not optimize solely for opens or clicks.
