- **Worst:** customer-interview invites ($0, three sends), "5x smaller particles than regular creatine." ($0 on 16,078 despite a 71% open), "No bloat. No breaks." ($0.002), "+2.4 lb lean tissue with no workout program" ($0.008), "Why women 40+ are taking creatine." ($0.008), scientist-joins sends ($0.008–0.014). **Shared:** mechanism/science or tech-spec claims with no offer and no clear next step; strong opens, almost no clicks.

**Takeaways**
1. SMS is the account's best channel (SMS welcome at $1.40 RPR) and there were zero SMS campaigns in 60 days. Biggest easy gap.
2. Promo sends earn 3–4× the median; pure science sends earn little on their own. Education should lead into an offer sequence (education → proof → deadline), not stand alone.
3. 5.6 sends a week with 0.48% clicks: fewer, sharper emails with one obvious action will do more than volume.
4. Retention is the lever: the habit message ("4 gummies a day") is the best value angle and matches the form's LTV problem.
5. The Opensend welcome track reaches 40,943 people at $0.04 RPR; it needs its own shorter track and a sunset rule so it doesn't drag deliverability.

## 4. Voice
**Sender persona:** **Claire from Arq8** (proposed 2026-11-17, awaiting approval) — sends campaigns, flows and SMS sign-offs; founder notes stay from the founder (name needed). Why: a calm, clear, grown-up name that suits a 45+ audience and the "calm authority" voice; speaks for the team, never claims personal results.

How Arq8 talks (form):
- **Calm authority** — "Creatine never failed you. The delivery did." (home)
- **Mechanism before promise** — explain how (particle size, dissolving, saturation), then what.
- **Scientific, never academic** — plain words, one number at a time.
- **Short sentences, controlled rhythm** — "4 gummies a day. That is the routine."
- **Transparent** — say what creatine won't do and when results show (~28 days).
Words to use: daily, consistent, dissolve, absorb, strength, capable, proven, tested. Avoid: hype, "gains", "beast", "shred", miracle, detox, wellness clichés, fitness-bro energy.
Required: FDA disclaimer and "Individual results may vary" on every email with claims.
