**Takeaways**
1. **Product-guide and "which one is right for you" content to the 90-day engaged list is the consumer engine** (mushrooms, matcha guide, vanilla formats, beans vs instant: $0.69–0.94 RPR at ~60% opens).
2. **B2B is the highest-value audience per email** (co-packing $3.06 RPR; Matcha Wholesale flow $47 RPR). It deserves its own steady track.
3. **Widening to 180/365-day and full lists for Black Friday cut opens to 19–42% and RPR to $0.04–0.43.** The FREEMATCHA sends are the weakest of the period. Keep the core offer sends on the engaged list and use the wide audiences sparingly.
4. Reviews in the customer's own words work ($0.90). Re-sends to non-openers work (Nov 3, $1.77).
5. Plain-text founder notes from Jeff exist (the SMS invite, the co-pack follow-up) and fit the brand's voice.

## 3. Avatars

### Avatar 1: The health-conscious home buyer who's tired of overpaying
- **Who:** 30–55, slightly more women, very health-conscious (brief). Buys coffee, matcha, cacao, mushrooms for a daily ritual and their kitchen (site bestsellers; Klaviyo winners).
- **Problems they're facing:** Health products are overpriced and the quality is a gamble — "overpriced health products and low quality are the main pain points" (brief). They can't tell which matcha, mushroom or vanilla format they actually need (best campaigns were "which one is right" guides).
- **Circumstances they're in:** A daily routine built around coffee, matcha or smoothies; buying the same staples again and again at retail prices; reading labels in a market where some sellers mislabel and overclaim (brief: "mis-labeling, false claiming benefits, poor quality").
- **The desire:** "A reliable source to find high quality superfoods at reasonable prices" (brief) — a pantry they trust and don't overpay for.
- **What keeps them lying awake at night:** Not a true night-time fear for most. What drives them is the nagging suspicion of being overcharged for something that may not be what the label says. _(Treat as a strong frustration, not a fear; don't dramatize.)_
- **What they think of themselves:** Smart, label-reading, health-first; not someone who falls for wellness hype (inferred from brief tone; confirm with reviews).
- **Objections:** "Why is it so cheap — is something wrong with it?" (Klaviyo subject "How come your cacao costs so little?"); "Will a pound go stale before I finish it?"; which format to buy.
- **Their words:** _needs client reviews._
- **Current situation → desire:** paying boutique prices for staples they can't fully trust → a bulk pantry of verified-organic staples at a fair price, from a family they know.
- **Entry point:** Organic Instant Coffee, Culinary/Ceremonial Matcha, Cacao Powder.

### Avatar 2: The small café, juice or smoothie bar owner
- **Who:** Small food and beverage businesses — "a lot of our customers are small businesses like juice/smoothie bars and they need reliability" (brief). Wholesale-tagged customers and wholesale leads in Klaviyo; per-category wholesale flows (matcha, coffee, cacao, coconut).
- **Problems they're facing:** Inconsistent quality and supply from ingredient vendors; prices that eat their margins (brief).
- **Circumstances they're in:** Running a shop, reordering the same ingredients on a schedule, every drink depending on that ingredient tasting the same every time.
- **The desire:** One supplier they never have to think about: same quality, fair bulk price, there when they reorder (brief: "they need reliability").
- **What keeps them lying awake at night:** Running out mid-week or a batch that tastes different — their customers notice before they do. _(Applies: their business depends on it.)_
- **Objections:** Minimums, lead times, consistency between batches, wholesale pricing.
- **Current situation → desire:** juggling vendors and hoping this batch matches the last → one reliable organic supplier at wholesale prices.
- **Entry point:** Matcha (wholesale flow RPR $47), coffee, cacao, coconut.

### Avatar 3: The founder launching their own product (private label / co-packing)
- **Who:** Early-stage food, beverage and supplement brands looking for private label or co-manufacturing (site: get-a-quote form with packaging, SKUs, run volumes; Klaviyo: co-packing leads, "Your label. Our floor. From 100 units.").
- **Problems they're facing:** "Most co-packers won't talk to you" at small volumes (Klaviyo subject, Oct 2); high minimums lock out small runs.
- **Circumstances they're in:** Has a product idea or early brand, limited capital, needs formulation, packaging and a first run.
- **The desire:** Get their own branded product made at a small first run, with a partner who'll actually take the call.
- **What keeps them lying awake at night:** Sinking money into a big minimum order before they know the product sells. _(Applies.)_
- **Objections:** Minimums, cost per unit, R&D help, timelines.
- **Current situation → desire:** turned away by co-packers for being too small → their label on shelves from a 100-unit first run.
- **Entry point:** Private label quote.

## 4. Voice
- **Sender persona (proposed):** Hannah from Dolce — sends campaigns and flows; founder notes stay from Jeff Weaver. Pending client approval.
- **Brand voice:** "Like a nice handshake or a warm hug from a friend" (brief). Warm, personal, a little playful (emoji in subject lines, "Karen's not drinking her coffee. She's baking with it.") and plain-spoken.
- **Founder voice:** Jeff writes plain-text notes in first person ("I can't spill all the beans yet… Jeff Weaver, Dolce Superfoods") (Klaviyo).
- **Use:** quality, sourcing, family-run, fair price, organic, the math (cost per cup/serving — a CPA founder can own it).
- **Avoid:** health claims and benefit overclaiming (the brief names false claims as the industry's problem; keep it to sourcing and quality facts). **Never repeat the site's "FDA Approved"** (FDA doesn't approve foods/supplements).
