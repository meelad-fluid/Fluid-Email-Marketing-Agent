# El Viejon — creative direction (running summary)
_Updated 2026-11-23 from 5 past campaign designs (BFCM VIP x3, Halloween x2), onboarding answers, website and Klaviyo._

**Look**
- Two moods: all-black with flying embers and flames, or bright Ranch Gold. Most emails mix them (gold hero → fiery dark body → gold footer).
- Black pouches styled with red chiles, limes, habaneros, salt and pepper. Shown as a lineup on a wood table, tilted floating cutouts, or one hero pouch with ingredients bursting out.
- Lifestyle photos of adults laughing and sharing jerky, with dark overlay cards on top.
- Devices they use: wavy and flame-edge section transitions, small squiggle dividers, rounded gold cards that overlap sections, outlined benefit rows with flame icons.

**Type**
- Headlines: heavy condensed sans in ALL CAPS (Oswald-style), stacked over 2–3 lines and often ending with a period ("THE LIST SHOPS FIRST.").
- Body: clean sans (Inter-style), centered.
- Script tagline "Como en el Rancho." in the footer.

**Buttons**
- Pill shape, ALL CAPS, letter-spaced.
- Gold with black text on dark sections, black with gold text on gold sections.

**Recurring blocks**
- Testimonial card with a big quote mark, verified buyer name in letter-spaced caps.
- Product cards: pouch, white pill name label, one-line flavor description.
- Dashed offer pill: "20% OFF | CODE | ENDS DATE".
- Gold footer: "Hand made. Small batch. No preservatives. 100% family owned." + script tagline + socials.

**Tone**
- Direct, short sentences, talks to the reader's real moment ("The bag you finished too fast? Grab another.").
- Familia. Scarcity comes from small handmade batches, never fake urgency.
- Hosting angle for holidays: "the adults want something real to snack on."

**What's worked**
- Founder plain-text notes ("Palabras Del Viejon"), short punchy subjects, VIP early access.

**Offers in use**
- VIEJON10 welcome, 20% BFCM VIP (FAMILIA20, Nov 15–19), free shipping over $75, Sampler Pack for hosting.

**Avoid**
- Sombrero or maraca clichés, organic or health claims, a mass-market feel.
