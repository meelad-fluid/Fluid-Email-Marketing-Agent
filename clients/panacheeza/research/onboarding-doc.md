# Panacheeza — Onboarding Doc
_Prepared 2026-11-26 · Sources: onboarding answers, panacheeza.com, Klaviyo audit (last 60 days), Klaviyo order events (last 90 days / 365 days), public reviews and press_

**In one line:** a flavor-first, 5-ingredient dairy-free parmesan that people use up and rebuy, so email's job is trial → refill → subscribe.

---

## 1. What has worked (Klaviyo, last 60 days)

**Basics**
- **Lists:** Newsletter, SMS Subscribers, FC | SMS VIP List, plus B2B/outreach lists (keep those out of consumer sends)
- **Pop-ups:** desktop and mobile forms both live (FC | Pop-UP Form) · submit rate not in the audit
- **Sends:** 20 email campaigns + 2 SMS in 60 days · 2.3 emails a week
- **Engagement:** 56% average open · 0.94% average click (benchmarks 35% / 0.5%)
- **Audience sizes:** main engaged audience ~8,500 (60% opens) · full list ~15,000 (33–35% opens)
- **Sender:** hello@panacheeza.com · US/Eastern
- **Not in the audit:** master-sheet segments (FC | Engaged 90 etc.) were not found by name

**Flows (last 60 days)**
- **FC | Welcome Email** · email + SMS · $2,255 · $0.67 per recipient · 61 orders on 3,389 recipients
- **FC | Abandoned Checkout** · email + SMS · $697 · $0.88 RPR · 19 orders on 788
- **FC | Site Abandonment** · email · $433 · $0.21 RPR · 13 orders on 2,020
- **FC | Browse Abandonment** · email + SMS · $307 · $0.36 RPR · 9 orders on 845
- **FC | Post Purchase** · email · $254 · $0.07 RPR · 5 orders on 3,901
- **FC | BFCM Offer Code** · seasonal · $58 · 1 order
- **FC | Winback** · live · no attributed revenue in the window
- **Total flow revenue:** about $4,004 in 60 days

**Campaigns: best 5 by revenue per recipient**
- "Your free bag offer ends today" · VIP free-bag last call · $1,163 · $0.138 RPR
- "It is not just a pasta thing" · 6 uses beyond pasta · $1,020 · $0.119 RPR
- "Buy Any Bulk Bag, Get a FREE Gift!" · VIP early opening, full list · $1,773 · $0.119 RPR
- "Looks fancy. Takes 10 minutes." · bruschetta recipe · $937 · $0.110 RPR
- "Buy any bulk bag, get a free 1 lb bag" · stock-up offer · $787 · $0.093 RPR
- **What they share:**
  - A free bag beats money off: the free-bag offer out-earned the "$40 → $20 credit" BFCM offer by about 3x per recipient
  - Quick, impressive uses ("10 minutes", "6 ways") sell without a discount
  - Short, concrete subject lines

**Campaigns: worst 3**
- "Cozy White Mushroom Lasagna" · long recipe · $0.029 RPR (highest click rate, 1.5%, but few orders)
- "Your Thanksgiving sides, sorted" · full list of ~15k · $0.033 RPR
- "Pick your flavor before November 8" · pre-sale flavor guide · $0.033 RPR
- **What they share:**
  - Long recipes pull clicks to the recipe, not the product
  - Full-list sends halve open rates
  - Pre-sale "guides" get people to wait for the sale

**SMS (2 sends)**
- Free-bag early access · $1,058 · $0.52 per recipient · 28% click
- Free-bag final hours · $564 · $0.29 per recipient
- SMS earns ~4x email per recipient, but unsubscribes run 1.2–1.9% per send

**Takeaways that shape the plan**
1. Sell trial with quick uses, then sell the refill (bulk bag) with a free-gift mechanic
2. Keep campaigns on engaged audiences; full list only for real sales moments
3. Recipes: short, product-on-plate, one button to the product
4. Post-purchase and abandoned checkout are the two flows leaving the most money behind
5. SMS for offers only, 1–2 a week max

## 3. Avatars

### Avatar 1: The dairy-free Italian food lover who misses real parmesan
- **Who they are:** women 35–60 who can't do dairy (intolerant or sensitive) and grew up on Italian food (brief, reviews)
- **Problems they're facing:**
  - "Most of them have tried everything under the sun" (brief)
  - Alternatives taste like garlic powder, "fish food" or have "a bitter aftertaste" (category reviews)
- **Circumstances:** cooking pasta night for a family that still eats dairy, making two versions of dinner
- **The desire:** "the same parmesan experience as traditional parmesan" (brief)
- **What keeps them up at night:** Not applicable for this brand. What drives them: missing a food that feels like home, and not wanting to be "the difficult one" at the table.
- **What they think of themselves:** "I'm the one who has to bring her own cheese"
- **Objections:** "Will it actually taste like parmesan?" · "It's the most expensive one"
- **Their words:**
  - "Best Dairy Free Cheese Ever!" (Debbie, site)
  - "A non-dairy cheese that really tastes like parmesan!" (site review)
  - "It is a game changer, and tickles your taste buds just right." (Angelica, site)
- **Current situation → desire:** "I gave up parmesan" → "pasta night tastes like it used to"
- **Entry point:** Original 8oz shaker, then the 1 lb refill bag

### Avatar 2: The clean-label flexitarian who reads every ingredient list
- **Who they are:** plant-forward or "meat reducer" cooks, often not vegan. "The majority of our consumers are meat eaters." (Janessa, Green Matters)
- **Problems they're facing:**
  - Plant-based cheeses full of starches, oils and additives (brief: "chemical enriched alternatives")
  - Nutritional yeast alone doesn't taste like parmesan (category reviews)
- **Circumstances:** shopping Whole Foods, flipping packs to read labels, cooking healthier for the family
- **The desire:** a pantry staple they don't have to second-guess
- **What keeps them up at night:** Not applicable. What drives them: feeling good about what goes on the family's plate.
- **Objections:** "Is it really only 5 ingredients?" · "Is it worth the price?"
- **Their words:**
  - "Ingredients You Can Pronounce" (site headline they respond to)
  - "sort of thick and a little buttery too" (site review)
  - "rich, cheesy, and slightly nutty" (category review)
- **Current situation → desire:** "I put the jar back after reading the label" → "the one I don't have to check"
- **Entry point:** Original shaker or Tasting Trio

### Avatar 3: The household stocker who goes through it fast
- **Who they are:** heavy users and families who shake it on everything, refill the shaker from bulk bags (order data: 1 lb and 3 lb reorder paths)
- **Problems they're facing:**
  - The 8oz shaker runs out "faster than expected" (category review on 8oz sizes)
  - Running out mid-week
- **Circumstances:** pasta, popcorn, roasted veg, salads several times a week; multiple eaters
- **The desire:** never run out, pay less per ounce
- **What keeps them up at night:** Not applicable. What drives them: convenience and value.
- **Objections:** "Will a big bag stay fresh?" (answer: shelf-stable 18 months unopened; refrigerate after opening)
- **Their words:**
  - "We started with the smaller size and quickly went to this to refill the canister" (Debbie, site)
  - "economical" and cuts down on plastic (1 lb bag reviews, paraphrase)
- **Current situation → desire:** "buying a shaker every two weeks" → "a bag in the pantry, a subscription that just shows up"
- **Entry point:** 1 lb Bulk Bag → 3 lb Bundle → subscribe & save

## 6. Competitors
- **Follow Your Heart:** grated parm that taste tests call closest to parmesan. We win on 5 ingredients and flavors.
- **Violife:** wedge format that divides tasters. We win on ready-to-shake and flavor.
- **Madly Hadley:** cashew parm in Whole Foods, smaller sizes. We win on flavors and bulk refills.
- **Farmer Foodie:** flavored blends (Golden Chedda, Italian Herb). We win on true parmesan taste.
- **Nooch It:** "cleanest ingredients" cashew parm, but "not a parmesan copycat." We win on taste plus a clean label.

**Our lane:** the one that tastes like nonna's parmesan and still has only 5 ingredients.
