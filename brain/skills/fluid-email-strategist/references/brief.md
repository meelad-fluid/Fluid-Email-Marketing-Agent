# The brief: what every send on the calendar carries

## Fluid's campaign brief (always these six, in this order)

Every email campaign brief starts with exactly these fields; this is what the team and the client read.

```
Email Campaign name:
Sent to:
Discount (if applicable):
Angle:
Goals:
Excluding:
```

- **Email Campaign name**: 2–6 words, how the team will refer to it ("Why it costs less").
- **Sent to**: a real segment or list in the client's Klaviyo, by its name, plus who that is (engaged 90d, non-buyers, lapsed…).
- **Discount (if applicable)**: code, amount, deadline, exclusions, within the client's max discount; "None" for value sends. Proposed until the client approves.
- **Angle**: the specific argument this email makes, in a sentence: the avatar's situation → the desire, and how the product gets them there. Not a genre ("educational") and not a product name.
- **Goals**: what the reader should understand or feel by the end, the one action, and the production note (designed or plain text, hero, key sections). Specific enough that two copywriters would write the same email.
- **Excluding**: who must not get it (recent purchasers in the last N days, people in the welcome or abandonment flows, unengaged 90d+, anyone who got the previous send, subscribers for a first-order offer…). "None" only if truly none.

The fields below are the strategist's working detail behind those six (send date/time, channel, format, why it's on the calendar, test). Keep them in the pipeline; lead with the six.

## Working detail

Each approved send becomes one pipeline entry at status **Brief**. The copywriter writes from it and the designer picks the template from it, so a missing field means someone has to go ask. Every field below is required unless marked optional.

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Send date and time** | In the client's timezone, with a send window for SMS ("9-11am ET") |
| **From** | The client's sender persona ("Hannah from Dolce"); the real founder only for a founder note |
| **Channel** | Email or SMS. A paired email + SMS is two entries; note the pairing on both |
| **Name** | 2-6 words saying what the email is about, the way the team would refer to it: "Why it costs less", "How to check your superfoods". Not the angle, and not the whole argument |
| **Monthly angle** | Which of the month's 3-5 angles this send belongs to, by its name |
| **Campaign type** | One of the playbook's types (single benefit focus, trending / best sellers, reviews, how it's made, product guide, back in stock, new product drop, holiday, growth update) or sale / promo |
| **Message** | The overarching message: the specific argument this send makes, in a sentence or two. Not a genre |
| **Sent to** | A real segment (or the playbook's standard one to build), and who it's for: engaged, lapsed or non-buyers |
| **Format** | Designed or plain text (designed for sales, plain text for value; a founder note needs a real reason to exist) |
| **Goal** | Audience, what they should understand or feel by the end, the one action, and the production instruction. Specific enough that two copywriters would write recognisably the same email |
| **Offer** (optional) | Code, amount, deadline, exclusions. Must sit within the client's max discount |
| **Why it's on the calendar** | The figure or playbook gap that earned the slot |
| **Test** (optional) | The one variable to test on this send, if it's one of the month's 2-3 tests |

## Goal: the field most often written uselessly

"Drive revenue" is true of every email ever sent and constrains nothing.

- **Bad:** Goal: drive sales of matcha.
- **Good:** Goal: engaged buyers who've only bought coffee learn that our ceremonial and culinary matcha are for different drinks, so they stop guessing. One action: shop the matcha pair. Designed, two product blocks, the comparison as the hero.

- **Bad:** Goal: re-engage lapsed customers.
- **Good:** Goal: customers who last ordered 90-180 days ago see the one thing that changed since (the new 1kg bag cuts cost per cup by a third) and reorder their usual. One action: reorder. Plain text from Jeff, one link, the price-per-cup number in the second sentence.
