# Copy models (internal)

Emails Fluid's team chose as the standard for how copy should speak to a person (Meelad, 2026-11-24). Each one is broken down into the move that makes it work. **Brands and codes are anonymized on purpose: learn the move, never reuse a line, product name, claim or offer, and never mention these anywhere a client could see** (same rule as the reference library).

Read the 3–5 closest to the email you're writing (same type: sale sequence, plain text, value, launch, gift, flow).

## Sale sequences: one offer, a new idea every send

**Bulk superfoods brand, buy-one-get-one on matcha (about 12 sends).**
- *WANTED poster*: subject "WANTED: 4 matchas" / preview "Bring them in & get one FREE". Each product is a suspect with an alias and a charge: "Name: Culinary, alias Cookie Jar Jack. Wanted for: sneaking into cookies, brownies and muffins." Steps become "How to report a sighting". Buttons: "Bring In The Suspect". **Move:** a device turns a rules-heavy offer into something people read for fun.
- *The bakers' bag*: "One bag is $23.99. With the code, two bags are $23.99. That's two pounds for every cookie, cake and smoothie on your list, with plenty left over for January." Subject "Pay for 1 bag. Bake with two." **Move:** one product, one audience, the maths done in their kitchen.
- *Final 3 days*: "It's Sunday, which leaves you today, Monday and Tuesday to get your two bags and pay for just one." **Move:** the deadline in the reader's week, not a countdown.
- *Founder note, Small Business Saturday*: "I spent six months in Madagascar learning the vanilla business… We do our best to keep prices low all year. That's also why our Black Friday deal is rare: there isn't much room left to give anything away." **Move:** the story explains why the offer is real.
- *Founder note, last night*: subject "We're about to start charging for the second bag". "My personal favorite is the Ceremonial. But if you've got a real sweet tooth, the Latte Blend is your bag." **Move:** a person's recommendation, not a grid.
- *Early access to non-openers*: "Need help getting to those $160? Basket #1 will get you to $184.98" with the products listed. **Move:** remove the work between them and the threshold.

**37mm launcher brand, one 10% code plus a free-rounds code.**
- Thanksgiving: a place card, "Reserved: whoever brought the launcher. Eat first. Then everybody files out to the backyard, points it at open sky, and the night gets a whole lot more memorable." Preview: "Save the rounds for after dinner." **Move:** the product inside the reader's actual holiday.
- Black Friday: "No line, no campout." A deli ticket: "NOW SERVING: #1, you." **Move:** contrast with the Black Friday everyone knows.
- Launch day: an APPROVED stamp across a requisition form. Last day: a lost-and-found box, "Your 10% off, still unclaimed." Cyber Monday: a terminal that types the codes and prints the rack.
- Founder note: "[Founder] here. Black Friday in the shop is a strange one. Phones going, boxes moving, somebody's always hunting for packing tape. Good kind of chaos." **Move:** one real scene, then two codes explained in plain words.
- Every send: both codes, how they combine, the exact end with time zone, exclusions once.

**Creatine gummy brand, tiered discount.**
- "Pick how many months you want." Three tiles: 30, 60, 90 days, each with its gifts. **Move:** the decision framed as their supply, not pack counts.
- "90 days from now lands you in February. Start in December and January is just week 5." **Move:** the calendar does the persuading.
- Gift email: "For the person who will not take pills." "Nothing to swallow. Four gummies, chewed. Nothing to mix. No scoop, no shaker, no sink." **Move:** the recipient's objection is the headline.

**Tattoo-ink brand, 30% sitewide.**
- "Count the grays on your tray right now. Three? Four? A black you've thinned into a wash?" Then the set opens up what's missing, each family with one line: "For skin, stone and aged metal." / "For steel, shadow and cold light." **Move:** start at their station.
- "Meet [the red]. A red that's earned its place in 6 of our sets." Body: "Think of the last time a client showed you a reference with a lot of red in it, and you thought 'I wonder which red carries the piece.'" Section: "What you'll tattoo with it: roses and peonies, traditional work, small bold pieces." **Move:** one hero product through the artist's real jobs.
- "5 bestsellers from last year's Black Friday" / "Here they are again." Product line: "Line it, shade it, pack it, thin it into a wash. [The black] does all four from the same bottle."
- Last day: every sticker says "30% off today. Full price tomorrow." Sets email: "You save $470.85 on the 1oz."

**Custom decal printer, 15% sitewide.**
- Gift email built on recipients: "For the parents who say 'don't get me anything'" (a family photo magnet on the fridge), "For the kid who needs a little reminder they're doing well" (their name on the wall), "For the friend who bakes for everyone" (labels for cookie tins).
- Product lines in the buyer's world: "Your trailer follows your truck to every site. Stick some decals on the side, so it looks like it belongs on the site." · "On for the job, off when you're done."
- Ends with self-sorting buttons: "My Crew Needs Hard Hat Stickers", "I Need Labels for My Products", "Or Request A Quote".
- Last-call plain text: subject "We hate these kind of emails". "We'll be honest: we hate 'last chance' emails. But we'd hate it more if you missed this and we never said a word."

## Plain text with a personality

**DTF print-supply founder.**
- "No, I won't do it this time": "I have a reputation for doing this. If you know me, you know I like to extend our sales. It's practically my hobby. But not tonight." Then three picks, each one line of what it fixes. P.S. carries the exclusion.
- "hey, you awake?" / "I'm up too. Wanted to tell you something." "Tomorrow we all get back to work and you'll forget about our sale." Picks: "Image Remover: a mistake doesn't have to cost you a shirt." · "Cleaning Solution: lines in your prints? Dried ink in the nozzles?"
- "Thanksgiving confession…" / "(it's about how we got our name)": the pun from a get-together that became the brand. Offer only in the P.S.
- Value email: "3 mistakes that are quietly killing your booth sales" / "The vendors packing up at 2pm are all making the same ones." Pricing too low, playing safe with designs, stiff transfers ("Quality is felt before it's seen. A customer runs their fingers over the design and decides in two seconds."). Only the third mistake is the product. **Move:** teach their trade; sell one fix.

**Family jerky founder notes.** "People keep hitting reply. Telling me about the jerky they grew up on. The carniceria their dad took them to." Ends with the same question every time: "What did you grow up on? Hit reply." Each has a marked slot for one true detail from the founder's week, never invented.

**Decal reorder email.** "One goes on the truck. One on the toolbox. One on the laptop. And suddenly you're hunting for more places to stick them." Then why reordering is easy.

**Telehealth reward reminder.** Subject "you know you got paid, right?" / preview "$20". Two short paragraphs and a sign-in link.

## Value and seasonal emails

**Family jerky brand, Halloween week.**
- "Sweet is done. Bring the heat." / "Three pieces in, your mouth starts asking for the opposite. Salty. Chile forward. Something with teeth."
- "You did the costumes. You did the candy." Section: "The night after the night: tomorrow the house is wrecked, there's a bowl of leftover sugar nobody wants, and you finally sit down. That's the one to plan for."
- "Their ideas, not ours. We pulled these out of actual reviews." Each section is a review ("My Marine son takes a bag or 2 to the field") plus one line ("It doesn't melt, crush or need a cooler").

**Bulk superfoods brand.**
- "This holiday, turn your cookies blue. On purpose." / "Then, when someone asks what's in the frosting, tell them it's algae." Three recipes, each with its own button ("Make Blue Frosting").
- "Hot or cold, this creamer works for every cup of coffee you make." Two short recipes; buttons "Add This Cream To My Coffee" and "I'll Need The Coffee Too".

**Telehealth brand, social proof.** "He almost wrote us off as a scammer." A public forum thread told in three beats (found the site, nearly dismissed it, someone who ordered answered), typos kept, forum unnamed. Then: "We're not going to argue with the pricey part. The ID upload is deliberate. That's what keeps it legal." Button: "See what he was looking at."

## SMS

"[Brand]: October's booking up. We make every bag to order, so grab your Sampler now and skip the scramble: {url}" (honest urgency from how the product is made; about 100 characters before the link).
