# The customer research doc

**If `clients/<slug>/research/onboarding-doc.md` exists, that IS this doc** (built by `fluid-client-onboarding`): use its avatars, voice and conflicts, and only add what's missing. Otherwise build it as below.

Built at onboarding, before the first email is written, and kept alive: every new review theme, reply, rejection comment or campaign learning gets added. It's the foundation of every email, not a step to get through. Steps from Fluid's Copywriting SOP. Store it in the client's brain as `client_docs` kind `customer-research` (until that table exists: `clients/<slug>/customer-research.md`).

**Sources, in order:** the brief / onboarding answers, the brand guidelines, the live website (home, product pages, about, FAQ, reviews), the client's past emails, reviews and comments on their socials or review platform. Cite the source next to each answer.

## 1. Website interrogation

A list of conflicts between what the client told us and what the live site says. Each line: what the brief says · what the site says · where · why it matters for copy.

Check at least: hero product and best sellers; prices, bundles and subscription terms; the current offer and its exclusions; claims and proof (reviews count, certifications, guarantees, shipping and returns); who the site is clearly written for; the tone.

Conflicts go to the Fluid team as questions. Until answered, write around them (don't state the disputed fact).

## 2. Voice spec

- **How the brand talks**: 3–5 adjectives with a real example line from the site for each; sentence length; formality; humour; words it uses for the product and the customer.
- **How the customers talk**: 10–20 phrases lifted from reviews, comments and FAQs, verbatim, grouped by theme (the problem, the moment they noticed it worked, the objection they had). These are the words the copy reuses.
- **Never say**: banned words from the profile, competitor names, claims the brand can't make.

## 3. Avatar

One avatar per real customer type the brief or the pop-up answers point to (often matching the three pop-up options). Answer only what applies; write **not applicable** for the rest (a dog toy doesn't keep anyone up at night).

## 4. Their world (per avatar): the bank every email draws from

The avatar table says who they are. This says **where they are and what they say** when the product matters, which is what the copy is actually made of. Build it at onboarding, keep adding to it (every reply, review theme and rejection comment), and draw every email's "Talking to" line and idea from it.

| Section | What goes in it | Example (anonymized) |
|---|---|---|
| **What they do** | The role or activity they're in when they buy or use it, in their terms | Tattoo artist setting up a station · market vendor on a booth weekend · the parent who hosts |
| **Moments** (8–12) | Scenes you could photograph: when, where, what they're doing, what they say to themselves, what they've already tried | Packing up at 2pm with half the rack still full: "Why is nobody stopping?" |
| **Calendar and rituals** | The moments of their year and week when this product matters (holidays, seasons, game days, the end of a bag, back-to-work days) | The night after Halloween · November Sundays · the week before a convention |
| **Their words** (40–60 lines) | Verbatim phrases from reviews, comments, FAQs, replies, Reddit/Amazon threads for the category, competitor reviews; grouped by theme (the problem, the doubt, the moment it worked, how they use it). Typos kept | "I place at least 2-3 orders a month" · "it tastes like home" |
| **Trade and insider language** | Words their world uses that outsiders don't (for pros, their craft vocabulary) | Heal true to hue · packing · a wash · banding · nozzles |
| **How they actually use it** | Uses, pairings, recipes and hacks from real customers, including the ones the brand didn't plan | Takes a bag to field training · mixes it into iced coffee |
| **Doubts and objections** | What stops them, in their words, and the honest answer we're allowed to give | "Looks like a scam" · "pricey" · "will it arrive too tight?" |
| **Who they buy for** | Gift recipients and other people in their life the product touches | "My Marine son" · the parent who says "don't get me anything" |

Rules:
- **Sources**: the onboarding answers, the site and its reviews, the client's past emails and replies, social comments, and the wider category (Reddit, Amazon and competitor reviews via WebSearch/WebFetch). Mark each line's source. Never invent a quote and attribute it.
- **Enough to write from**: at least 40 verbatim lines across the themes before the first email. Thin research produces copy about the product, because there's nothing else to write about.
- **For professional buyers**, the trade language and the work moments matter more than feelings: speak their craft.

## Never invent

An answer you can't source stays blank with "needs client input". A made-up pain point produces confident copy about the wrong problem.
