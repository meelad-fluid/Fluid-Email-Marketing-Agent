# Choosing a framework

The four frameworks in Fluid's Copywriting SOP (definitions and examples: `knowledge/sops/copywriting-sop.md`). The SOP names PAS, AIDA and BAB for plain-text emails; the 4Ps fits designed promotional emails best. The "when not to" lines are the SOP's.

| Framework | Shape | Use it for | Don't use it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PAS**: Problem, Agitation, Solution | Name the problem → make it felt → the product solves it | Problem-led value emails, browse/cart objection emails, founder notes about why the brand exists, anything where the avatar has a clear pain | The product needs a long technical explanation, or there's no specific problem |
| **BAB**: Before, After, Bridge | Their life now → their life after → how they get there | Transformation products (supplements, beauty, fitness), welcome #1's chosen-benefit paragraph, winback ("what's changed"), post-purchase "how to get the result" | There's no clear benefit, or the product is complex/technical |
| **AIDA**: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action | Hook → why it matters → why this one → do it | Converting new or cold readers: welcome offer emails, product launches, site-abandonment #1 | Pure brand awareness; readers who already know the brand (use PAS); high-end/complex products that need trust built slowly |
| **4Ps**: Picture, Promise, Proof, Push | Show it → what it does for them → evidence → urgency | Designed sales emails: sale/offer campaigns, welcome #5 upgrade, cart mystery discount, bundles | Value-first content (the 80%), where a hard push breaks the trust the email is building |

## Mapping to Fluid's emails

| Email | Usual framework | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome #1 (per pop-up answer) | AIDA, with BAB for the chosen-benefit section | New reader; their answer gives you the "before" |
| Welcome #2 founder note (plain text) | PAS or BAB | The founder's own problem → why they built it |
| Welcome #3 reviews | 4Ps (proof-heavy) | Proof is the email |
| Welcome #5 upgrade offer | 4Ps | Push is the email |
| Site abandonment #1 | AIDA | They don't know the brand yet |
| Browse #1 / Cart #2 | PAS | Answer the doubt that stopped them |
| Cart #3–#4 mystery discount | 4Ps | Urgency |
| Post-purchase #1 / #3 (plain text) | BAB (soft) | Their life with the product; ask how it's going |
| Post-purchase #2 how-to | BAB | Using it right is the bridge to the result |
| Winback #1 | BAB | What's changed since they left |
| Value campaigns (the 80%) | PAS or BAB | Start from the reader's lived problem (e.g. "why you wake up bloated"), product second |
| Sales campaigns (the 20%) | 4Ps or AIDA | Offer-led |

These are defaults. The brief's angle wins; say in your notes which framework you used and why.
