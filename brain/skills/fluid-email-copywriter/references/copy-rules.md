# Fluid copy rules

Collected from Fluid's own documents. Where they disagree, the Design SOP wins for email copy structure, and the client's hard rules (banned claims, disclaimers, discount limits) win over everything.

Sources: `knowledge/sops/email-design-sop.md` (SOP), `brain/skills/fluid-email-strategist/references/playbook.md` (playbook), `brain/design/fluid-email-designer/references/design-rules.md` (design rules), Fluid's flow strategy template (strategy doc).

## Above the fold

- **Headline** names a specific customer pain point or problem (SOP). Short and punchy, usually 2-4 lines at email size (design rules). Exceptions: on sale emails the sale is the headline; on upgrade and last-call emails the offer numerals are the headline.
- **Subheadline**: 1-2 sentences. Usually where the offer is stated (design rules).
- **Offer**: the code, discount or deal, in the subheadline or as the code pill under the button (design rules). Always with a real deadline.
- **Button**: states the action *and* the solution (SOP). One primary button above the fold. The first CTA sits within two sentences of the opening copy (SOP).

## The body

- Paragraphs of 1-2 sentences (SOP). People skim.
- Features and benefits as bullets or checklists, **three per section** at most (SOP: the brain processes sets of three).
- Each section has its own short heading (design rules) and supports the hero; close with the offer and a button again (design rules).
- **Product blocks carry the product name and a button only** (design rules). No descriptions, no "BESTSELLER" labels. Guidance cards and reviews keep their text.
- Default skeleton when nothing else applies (SOP): pain-focused headline → two-sentence educational bridge → prominent CTA → three-point benefit checklist → final solution-oriented CTA.

## Subject lines and preview text (playbook)

- Under 50 characters. Curiosity or specificity. Personalize when it's natural, not as a tic.
- House examples: *5 ways to style our jeans* · *Our #1 best seller is back* · *This sold out in 48 hours* · *Your fall favorites are here*.
- **Preview text complements the subject, never repeats it**: *And why everyone's buying it* · *See what customers are saying*.
- **Words that push mail to Promotions or spam.** The test: would a friend send this word to another friend?
  - High risk: SALE (especially in caps), FREE TRIAL, BUY NOW, OPEN NOW, LIMITED TIME, URGENT, ACT NOW, EXCLUSIVE OFFER, CLICK HERE, SHOP NOW
  - Use carefully: discount, offer, deal, promotion, special, save, off
  - Also: all-caps words, multiple exclamation marks, excessive emoji, `$$$`, anything misleading
  - The conversational version is almost always better: "SALE: 50% OFF TODAY ONLY" → "Here's something we think you'll love."

## Plain-text emails (playbook, flow designs)

- They read like a person wrote them: greeting, the point, one or two text links, sign-off from a real name. No buttons, no images.
- **A founder note needs a real reason to exist**: something only the founder can say (a batch that turned out differently, a decision and why, a customer letter). If you can't name it, it's a designed email with a different angle.
- Invite replies ("A real person reads these").

## SMS (playbook)

- **One idea per message.** If it needs three points, it's an email.
- **Pair, don't duplicate**: the shortest true version of the same offer as its email.
- **A link to the exact thing it describes**, never just the homepage.
- Name the send window in the client's timezone.
- Compliance text (opt-out, brand prefix) is added by Klaviyo's settings; don't add it twice.

## Flow emails (strategy doc)

Each flow email has a fixed job; write to it. Examples:

- **Welcome 1**: welcome, the code and its value, the brand mission, the benefit they picked in the pop-up, best sellers for that pick.
- **Abandoned checkout 1**: a simple reminder to get them back to the cart; headline, subheadline, button, their items, button. No discount yet.
- **Abandoned checkout 3**: excitement and urgency; the mystery discount ("get up to X% off"), code MYSTERY.
- **Post-purchase 1**: from the founder; thank them for trusting the brand, build a personal connection, offer support. Plain text.
- **Win-back 1**: a check-in, not a hard sell.

The full jobs per email are in each flow blueprint (`brain/design/fluid-email-designer/references/flows/`) and framework (`brain/flows/`).

## Sound like a person, not AI (every client, standing rule from Fluid)

Write the way the client's customers actually talk: full, natural sentences that roll off the tongue when read out loud.

- **No fragment pairs or clipped punch lines.** Bad: "A free pound of matcha. Ends tomorrow." / "One tap. It's in your cart. Code included." / "Sorted." Good: "Stock up before Thursday and the matcha is on us." / "Each button adds everything to your cart and applies the code for you."
- **No two- or three-word sentences used for rhythm or drama.** One short sentence is fine when a person would really say it ("Talk soon.").
- **No AI tells:** colon or dash reveals ("The secret? Bulk."), "Here's the thing", triplets of one-word beats ("Clean. Simple. Organic."), "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- **Headlines are sentences too.** A friend should be able to say the headline out loud without it sounding like an ad.
- **Test:** read every line out loud. If a customer wouldn't say it that way in a text to a friend, rewrite it.
- Use the customers' own words from reviews, FAQs and comments where you have them (voice spec).
