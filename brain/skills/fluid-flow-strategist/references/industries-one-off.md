The system in `one-off-system.md` is the same for every category here. What changes is the pop-up question, what Post-Purchase #2 teaches, how big the offers can be, and how long the winback window runs. Read the section for the client's category, then the matching example for depth and voice.

## Brand facts to collect, every category

Same list as the replenishable notes (mission, founder, reason to believe, trust signals, guarantee, free-shipping threshold, support contact), plus what matters most for one-off purchases:

- Sizing and fit guides, exchange and return policy
- Materials, warranty, lifetime upgrade or repair programs
- Ongoing offers (Buy 2 Get 1, bundle tiers) and which codes can't be combined
- Discount exclusions: high-ticket items, MAP-restricted brands, custom quotes, sale items
- Whether customers can talk to an expert before buying (phone, chat, reply-to-email)
- Delivery times for made-to-order, oversized or freight items

## Jewelry and accessories

- **Pop-up:** by category (chains / rings / bracelets) or style (Daily Belts: Classic & Timeless / Warm & Versatile / Modern & Bold, mapped to colorways).
- **Welcome #1:** the category's trust basics: materials, plating or karat, care, warranty, how to choose length or size. Lead with the look, not the discount.
- **Post-Purchase #2:** care that keeps it looking new, sizing help, how to layer and pair.
- **Post-Purchase #4:** pairing modules (chain + bracelet, statement + everyday) and "need another color?" for single-style hits.
- **Offers:** jewelry runs higher percentages at accessible price points (Gold Urban 20% → 25%) and smaller ones, or exclusions, at luxury price points. Ongoing bundle offers stay visible, never stacked.
- **Winback:** the docs used a 60–90 day "style refresh" window. Use the data.

## Apparel and fashion

- **Pop-up:** by category (Angel Brinks: Dresses / Sets & Outfits / Tops & Bottoms; Sanhouse: Bodysuits / Tops / Bottoms), by who it's for (LGNDRY Fit: Women's / Men's Activewear), or by occasion (Ellaé Lisqué: Birthday or celebration / Wedding or formal event / Vacation or getaway). Occasion questions let Welcome #1 sell the feeling of the moment.
- **Welcome #3:** reviews about fit, fabric and how it looks on, with customer photos.
- **Post-Purchase #2:** styling by product type (Angel Brinks: minimal accessories for embellished dresses, nightlife styling for catsuits, event styling for gowns), try-on and care guidance, exchanges.
- **Post-Purchase #3:** for occasion buys, "How did it go?" after the event, plus a review with a photo.
- **Post-Purchase #4:** complete the look (matching top or bottom, accessories), or a second look for the same trip or weekend.
- **Kids' sizes:** a "next size up" angle in winback, timed to how fast kids grow out of it (Orion Moto used 6–12 months for balance bikes).
- **Winback:** 90 days in most fashion docs. Use the data; check season against launch timing.

## Home and decor

- **Pop-up:** by product type or by the customer's setup (FencePot: "What type of fence do you have?"; Makuzo: "What kind of scents do you prefer?").
- **Post-Purchase #2:** installation, placement and care (FencePot's installation guide, Makuzo's candle care, Layered's "how to get the most out of your blanket").
- **Post-Purchase #4:** "complete the room": same collection, matching pieces, more of the same where it makes sense ("Need more FencePots?").
- **Delivery:** large or made-to-order items arrive late; say so in Post-Purchase #1 and keep #2 useful even before delivery.
- **Candles and other semi-consumables:** check the data in Step 3. If same-product repeat is strong, borrow the replenishable system's reorder logic for Post-Purchase #4 and Winback, and say you did.

## Gear, equipment and other high-ticket purchases

- **Pop-up:** by category or by what they need help with (Liquid Surf: Windsurfing / Kayaks & SUPs / Electric Watercraft; Drone Doctor: Buy A Drone / Repair My Drone / Upgrade My Fleet).
- **Expert help is the selling point.** Every email has a "reply or call before you buy" path; the copy is helpful, never pushy ("We'd rather help you buy right the first time").
- **Offers:** small percentages with exclusions (MAP-restricted brands, high-ticket items, custom quotes, commercial packages), dollar amounts (Orion $25 → $50), or no discount at all (Drone Doctor: a free expert recommendation, priority help).
- **Post-Purchase #2:** setup, safety, storage, transport, charging, maintenance, by product type.
- **Post-Purchase #4:** add-ons and accessories per category (paddles, PFDs, roof racks; batteries, chargers, cases).
- **Winback by segment:** consumable parts on a short cycle (45–60 days), retail around 120, high-ticket and commercial at 180.

## Custom and B2B-mixed

- **Pop-up:** by customer type (Decals.com: Individual / Business Owner / Event Planner), so business buyers can get a different Welcome #1 and a sales-assisted path.
- **Post-Purchase #2:** application or installation instructions for the custom product, then "your next project".
- **Watch the data:** wholesale and business orders distort first-order rankings; separate them where Hiro lets you.

## Digital products

- No shipping, so Post-Purchase #1 confirms access and #2 is "how to drop it into your project" (Ovani Sound: game audio).
- Cross-sells are the next pack or bundle; the docs ran higher second-purchase codes here (Ovani: 25%) because there's no product cost per unit.

## Regulated products

Products with legal requirements (Exotic Ammo's 37mm launchers and ammunition) carry: a "shop responsibly, review product requirements before purchase" line in the pop-up confirmation and SMS; reminders about lawful use and safety instructions; no claims beyond what the client's site states; and any age or shipping-restriction requirements the client gives you, applied as flow rules. Follow the client's compliance guidance. Don't invent legal statements.
